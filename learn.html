01. SCIENCE

UNDERSTANDING SCIENCE

Science is a systematic approach to understanding the natural world through observation, experimentation, measurement and evidence.

MAJOR BRANCHES

Physics: Studies matter, energy, motion, forces and the fundamental laws governing physical systems.

Chemistry: Examines the composition, properties, structure and transformations of matter.

Biology: Investigates living organisms, their structures, functions, development and interactions.

SCIENTIFIC APPLICATION

Scientific knowledge supports the development of medicines, renewable energy technologies, communication systems and advanced materials.

PRACTICAL EXAMPLE

Physics helps engineers understand how forces affect a bridge, while chemistry helps scientists investigate the properties of construction materials.

02. DIGITAL SKILLS

WHAT ARE DIGITAL SKILLS?

Digital skills are the abilities required to use digital devices, applications and online platforms effectively, safely and responsibly.

ESSENTIAL COMPETENCIES

Computer Literacy: Understanding how to operate computers, manage files and use common applications.

Internet Literacy: Finding, evaluating and using online information while recognizing unreliable sources.

Programming: Using programming languages and logical instructions to develop software and automate tasks.

Digital Security: Understanding passwords, privacy, secure browsing and responsible online behaviour.

REAL-WORLD APPLICATION

A student can use spreadsheets to organize data, presentation software to communicate research and programming tools to develop simple applications.

03. MATHEMATICS

THE ROLE OF MATHEMATICS

Mathematics is the study of numbers, quantities, structures, patterns and relationships. It provides essential methods for analysing problems and developing logical solutions.

MAJOR AREAS

Algebra: Uses symbols and equations to represent mathematical relationships and unknown quantities.

Geometry: Studies shapes, dimensions, angles, areas and spatial relationships.

Statistics: Provides methods for collecting, analysing, interpreting and presenting data.

Calculus: Studies change and accumulation, with applications in physics, engineering and economics.

ENGINEERING APPLICATION

Engineers use mathematical models to estimate forces, calculate electrical quantities, analyse structures and predict system behaviour.

04. GENERAL KNOWLEDGE

WHY GENERAL KNOWLEDGE MATTERS

General knowledge provides a broad understanding of subjects beyond a person's specialist field. It supports informed thinking, communication and awareness of the wider world.

AREAS TO EXPLORE

History: Examines past events, societies and developments that have shaped the modern world.

Geography: Studies Earth's physical features, environments, populations and human activities.

Economics: Examines how individuals and societies allocate resources, produce goods and provide services.

Current Affairs: Develops awareness of important events and developments in society.

LEARNING HABIT

Read reliable educational materials regularly, compare information from trustworthy sources and remain curious about unfamiliar subjects.
