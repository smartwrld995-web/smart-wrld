01. MECHANICAL ENGINEERING

FIELD OVERVIEW

Mechanical engineering involves the application of engineering mechanics, thermodynamics, fluid mechanics and materials science to the development of machines and mechanical systems.

CORE AREAS OF STUDY

Engineering Mechanics: The study of forces, motion and the behaviour of physical bodies under different loading conditions.

Thermodynamics: The study of heat, energy, temperature and their transformation into useful work.

Machine Design: The process of developing mechanical components and systems that satisfy specified performance, safety and reliability requirements.

Manufacturing Engineering: The application of processes and technologies used to convert raw materials into finished products.

INDUSTRIAL APPLICATIONS

Mechanical engineers contribute to automotive production, aerospace systems, power generation, industrial automation, manufacturing and heating and cooling systems.

REAL-WORLD EXAMPLE

An automotive engineer may analyse the forces acting on a vehicle's suspension system to improve stability, comfort and durability.

ENGINEERING INSIGHT

Mechanical engineering connects physics, mathematics and creativity to solve real-world mechanical problems.

02. ELECTRICAL ENGINEERING

FIELD OVERVIEW

Electrical engineering focuses on the generation, transmission, distribution and application of electrical energy. It also includes the design and analysis of electronic and control systems.

CORE AREAS OF STUDY

Circuit Theory: The analysis of voltage, current, resistance and power in electrical networks.

Power Systems: The study of electrical generation, transmission, distribution and protection.

Electronics: The design and application of components such as diodes, transistors, integrated circuits and electronic devices.

Control Systems: The study of systems designed to regulate the behaviour and output of machines and industrial processes.

INDUSTRIAL APPLICATIONS

Electrical engineers work in renewable energy, telecommunications, industrial automation, power utilities, electronics manufacturing and electrical infrastructure.

REAL-WORLD EXAMPLE

An electrical engineer may design a solar power system that converts sunlight into electrical energy and manages its distribution to connected equipment.

ENGINEERING INSIGHT

Electrical engineering helps power homes, industries, communication networks and modern technology.

03. CIVIL ENGINEERING

FIELD OVERVIEW

Civil engineering is concerned with the planning, design, construction and maintenance of the physical infrastructure required by society. It combines mathematics, structural analysis, materials science and environmental principles.

CORE AREAS OF STUDY

Structural Engineering: The analysis and design of structures to ensure they can safely resist applied loads and environmental conditions.

Geotechnical Engineering: The study of soil and rock behaviour to support foundations, retaining structures and other construction works.

Transportation Engineering: The planning and development of roads, highways, railways and transportation networks.

Environmental Engineering: The application of engineering methods to water treatment, waste management and environmental protection.

INDUSTRIAL APPLICATIONS

Civil engineers contribute to building construction, bridge development, highway projects, drainage systems, water supply networks and urban planning.

REAL-WORLD EXAMPLE

A structural engineer may calculate the loads acting on a bridge and select suitable materials and structural elements to meet safety and performance requirements.

ENGINEERING INSIGHT

Civil engineering helps create the infrastructure that connects communities and supports society.
